# Research Record: CMIP6 Uttaradit Follow-on Study

## Frozen plan (written before evidence collection)

### Research question

How should the data, code, and manuscript in `Future Changes Uttaradit.zip`, together with the two supplied reference papers, be improved and extended into a methodologically defensible new climate-impact article suitable for a current Q3 journal and constrained to no more than 15 pages?

### Hypotheses and falsifiers

- **H1 - The current workflow leaves a publishable methodological gap beyond a conventional ensemble-mean projection.** Prediction: the archive will omit at least one material element such as bias-correction validation, uncertainty decomposition, extremes analysis, model-independence weighting, or robustness testing. Falsifier: the code and manuscript already implement, validate, and report all of these elements transparently.
- **H2 - A focused extremes-and-uncertainty extension is more defensible than simply adding another location or scenario plot.** Prediction: the supplied studies will emphasize mean/seasonal changes more than compound or decision-relevant extremes, and the available data/code can support daily-scale extensions. Falsifier: the source studies already cover the same indices, uncertainty framework, scale, and decision question proposed for the extension.
- **H3 - The existing code requires reproducibility and statistical-validity improvements before journal submission.** Prediction: inspection or reruns will reveal hard-coded paths, unclear units/calendars, untested assumptions, incomplete provenance, or significance/ensemble handling issues. Falsifier: the repository is parameterized, documented, testable, reproducible, and its reported results can be independently recomputed without material discrepancy.
- **H4 - A paper of at most 15 pages is feasible if the contribution is narrowed to one primary scientific question and supplementary material carries secondary diagnostics.** Prediction: a compact structure can contain one main analysis pipeline, 4-6 main figures/tables, and explicit uncertainty/robustness evidence. Falsifier: the necessary evidence cannot be presented without either omitting validity checks or exceeding the page constraint.

### Methods and planned checks

- **M1 (confirmatory):** Inventory every archive member; classify source code, raw/processed data, figures, tables, environments, and manuscript assets; record sizes and hashes.
- **M2 (confirmatory):** Extract both PDFs completely, inspect document metadata and page renders, and build a structured comparison of question, data, baseline/future periods, scenarios, downscaling/bias correction, metrics, uncertainty treatment, validation, findings, and limitations.
- **M3 (confirmatory):** Review all executable code for I/O assumptions, calendars, units, missing values, spatial/temporal aggregation, model alignment, scenario/baseline consistency, statistics, plotting, provenance, and reproducibility.
- **M4 (confirmatory):** Profile supplied structured climate data at its actual grain and rerun high-impact calculations or smoke tests where dependencies and data permit.
- **M5 (confirmatory):** Search current primary literature and official journal/index sources for methodological novelty, comparable Thailand/SE Asia studies, and current journal scope/ranking evidence; do not treat a ranking inferred from title reputation as verified.
- **M6 (confirmatory):** Map each proposed code change to a scientific claim, validation test, expected manuscript output, and page-budget role.
- **M7 (confirmatory):** Produce a technical report with prioritized findings, one recommended article title/concept, alternatives, a <=15-page outline, and a staged implementation roadmap.
- **M8 (confirmatory):** Independently re-check the evidence-to-claim map and final recommendation without relying on the expected verdict.

### Stopping rules

Stop when all archive members and all relevant PDF pages have been inspected; high-impact code/data claims have reproducible evidence or are explicitly marked unverified; the literature search has primary/official support for the proposed novelty and journal-fit statements; every confirmatory method M1-M8 is closed; and the final verdict is not inconclusive. If a source is inaccessible or an essential computation cannot run, record the blocker and state exactly what is needed rather than substituting a weaker claim.

- **E003 (2026-08-31, M2 supplied PDFs):** All 12 pages of the 2023 Northeast Thailand paper and all 14 pages of the 2026 Thailand seasonal-rainfall paper were text-extracted and visually inspected. The first paper uses four CMIP5 GCMs and RCP4.5/RCP8.5 but does not disclose a reproducible bias-correction/calibration protocol or precipitation validation; its six-year comparison is also described in per-decade units and its RCP-ordering statements conflict between results and conclusion. The second paper uses ERA5 monthly precipitation (1995-2024), ONI, and four stations; it does not control gridwise multiplicity or report ENSO-phase sample sizes. Its seasonal components in Table 2 do not sum to the corresponding annual totals (El Nino: 1633.4 vs 1792.8 mm; Neutral: 1855.2 vs 1817.3 mm; La Nina: 1978.7 vs 1918.2 mm), and the text calls an MBE of -41.58 mm an overestimation although its stated convention is estimate minus observation. Observation: both sources motivate transparent validation, phase alignment, multiple-testing control, and explicit uncertainty rather than another descriptive mean-change paper.
- **E004 (2026-08-31, M1/M4 supplied data):** The archive contains result tables but no gauge time series, raw or extracted CMIP6 daily series, NetCDF files, SST/ENSO data, run manifest, or executable-run outputs needed to recompute the manuscript. Eleven CSV files and two XLSX files were profiled as 24 tabular objects; all were result/metadata tables. The largest supplied object is `S5_station_level_changes` (3,276 rows x 11 columns; 0.117% missing cells). Both workbooks contain static values and no formulas. `config/uttaradit.yaml` defines only a Near period (2021-2050), whereas `FINAL_ACCEPTANCE_GATES.csv`, `INTERPRETATION_SCOPE.csv`, and source scripts refer to Mid/Late periods and a quarantined SSP5-8.5 Late run. Observation: numerical paper results cannot be independently reproduced from the ZIP and some example outputs have unresolved run/config provenance.
- **E005 (2026-08-31, M3/M4 executable probes):** `python -m compileall -q` completed with exit code 0 over the extracted framework, but no test files were present. `analysis_output/audit_experiments.py` completed with exit code 0 and isolated three material behaviours: (1) `temporal_metrics` reported a 20-day wet spell across one missing day where a calendar-aware result is two 10-day spells; (2) `annual_indices` inserted 29 February into a no-leap five-day wet sequence and returned Rx5day=NaN and CWD=3 instead of 50 mm and 5 days; (3) QDM assigned probability 0.491667 to every value in the first 30-value tied block where the empirical midrank is 0.25. Observation: missingness, calendar handling, and ties can alter sequence and distribution diagnostics.
