# Literature research record: CMIP6 evaluation and province-scale projections for Uttaradit

Retrieval date: 2026-08-29 (Asia/Bangkok)

## Frozen plan (written before evidence collection; do not revise)

### Research question

Which peer-reviewed primary studies and authoritative assessment sources can support two distinct, realistic article concepts using the attached Uttaradit CMIP6 code/data: (1) scale-aware historical model evaluation and selection, and (2) bias-corrected future climate-change/extreme projections with province-level adaptation relevance?

### Hypotheses and falsifiers

- **H1 — Temporal-scale dependence.** Daily and monthly aggregation will emphasize different aspects of precipitation/temperature performance, so model skill or ranking may change with aggregation. **Prediction:** primary studies or methodological evidence will report aggregation-dependent metrics/rankings or warn that monthly validation does not establish daily/extreme skill. **Falsifier:** relevant evidence consistently shows rankings and conclusions invariant to daily versus monthly evaluation.
- **H2 — Bias correction and ensemble design.** Distribution-sensitive bias correction and performance-aware ensemble selection can improve historical distributional skill, but validation must be out-of-sample and climate-change-signal preservation must be assessed. **Prediction:** primary methodological studies will document both improvement and risks/assumptions. **Falsifier:** methods are shown not to affect relevant skill or change signals, or no validation requirements are reported.
- **H3 — Projection asymmetry.** CMIP6/AR6 evidence for Thailand/Southeast Asia will be more robust for warming than for mean precipitation; heavy-precipitation and heat extremes may nevertheless show actionable signals. **Prediction:** authoritative and primary regional studies will show high-confidence warming with larger precipitation spread/seasonal dependence. **Falsifier:** regional evidence shows equally robust, spatially uniform mean-precipitation direction and no relevant extreme changes.
- **H4 — Publishable gap.** A province-specific Uttaradit workflow jointly connecting daily/monthly model evaluation, transparent selection/weighting, validated bias correction, and future seasonal/extreme projections is uncommon. **Prediction:** the strongest literature will mostly be national, multi-country, basin, or regional rather than a matched Uttaradit province study. **Falsifier:** a peer-reviewed study already implements essentially the same end-to-end workflow for Uttaradit with comparable data and scenarios.

### Planned methods

- **M1 (confirmatory):** Search journal/publisher pages, DOI records, and authoritative IPCC/WMO/Thai sources for primary studies covering Taylor-diagram/model evaluation, temporal aggregation, bias correction/ensemble selection, CMIP6 SSP projections, climate extremes, and Thailand/province adaptation.
- **M2 (confirmatory):** Verify bibliographic metadata and claims against full abstracts/text or official assessment pages; record URL, publication date/year, search query, direct support, and caveat.
- **M3 (confirmatory):** Map evidence to H1–H4 and derive two non-overlapping article concepts, without asserting journal quartiles unless a current ranking source and year/system are verified.
- **M4 (confirmatory):** Independent verifier reviews the frozen plan, evidence log, and proposed conclusions without being told the desired verdict.

### Stopping rules

Stop when 8–12 strong sources collectively cover all six requested themes; at least two are directly relevant to Thailand/Southeast Asia; every material implication has an evidence ID; metadata is verified where accessible; dead ends and search limitations are recorded; and an independent verdict is obtained. If an independent agent slot remains unavailable, stop after the evidence threshold and explicitly mark M4 open and the verdict provisional.

## Evidence log

### E01 — Taylor diagram is a compact diagnostic, not a complete ranking method

- **Source:** Taylor, K. E. (2001), *Summarizing multiple aspects of model performance in a single diagram*, Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres 106(D7), 7183–7192. Official journal page: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1029/2000JD900719
- **Publication / retrieval:** first published 1 April 2001 / retrieved 29 August 2026.
- **Fingerprint:** “correlation, their root-mean-square difference, and the ratio of their variances.”
- **Direct support:** A Taylor diagram jointly displays pattern correlation, centered RMS difference, and relative variability, and can compare multiple models.
- **Caveat:** It does not show mean bias, wet-day frequency, distribution tails, nonstationarity, or application-specific loss. Treating closeness on the diagram as a universal scalar rank requires an additional, declared scoring rule.
- **Links:** H1, M1, article concept A.

### E02 — Daily distributions contain information omitted by mean/monthly skill

- **Source:** Martinez-Villalobos, C., Neelin, J. D., & Pendergrass, A. G. (2022), *Metrics for Evaluating CMIP6 Representation of Daily Precipitation Probability Distributions*, Journal of Climate 35(17), 5719–5743. Official AMS page: https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/clim/35/17/JCLI-D-21-0617.1.xml ; NOAA bibliographic record: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/64977
- **Publication / retrieval:** 1 September 2022 / 29 August 2026.
- **Fingerprint:** “society is not only adapted to mean conditions.”
- **Direct support:** The study evaluates 35 CMIP6 models using wet-day fraction, PDF shape, characteristic intensity scales, and probability-distance metrics; many models rain too often and too lightly. This supports a daily-distribution audit beyond monthly Taylor statistics.
- **Caveat:** Global/regional evaluation, not a direct daily-versus-monthly rank comparison for Thailand. Observational products themselves differ, so reference uncertainty should be acknowledged.
- **Links:** H1, article concept A.

### E03 — Conventional quantile mapping can distort projected changes; QDM was designed to preserve quantile changes

- **Source:** Cannon, A. J., Sobie, S. R., & Murdock, T. Q. (2015), *Bias Correction of GCM Precipitation by Quantile Mapping: How Well Do Methods Preserve Changes in Quantiles and Extremes?*, Journal of Climate 28(17), 6938–6959. Official AMS page: https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/clim/28/17/jcli-d-14-00754.1.xml
- **Publication / retrieval:** 1 September 2015 / 29 August 2026.
- **Fingerprint:** “quantile mapping can artificially corrupt future model-projected trends.”
- **Direct support:** The paper introduces QDM and demonstrates that standard QM can strongly inflate future changes in precipitation extremes and return values; QDM better preserves relative changes in precipitation quantiles.
- **Caveat:** Demonstration uses CMIP5 precipitation over Canada. Preserving quantile changes does not automatically preserve mean change, and split-sample validation remains necessary.
- **Links:** H2, both concepts.

### E04 — Performance weighting should also account for model dependence

- **Source:** Knutti, R., Sedláček, J., Sanderson, B. M., Lorenz, R., Fischer, E. M., & Eyring, V. (2017), *A climate model projection weighting scheme accounting for performance and interdependence*, Geophysical Research Letters 44, 1909–1918. Official journal page: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/2016GL072012
- **Publication / retrieval:** 8 February 2017 / 29 August 2026.
- **Fingerprint:** “accounts both for the large differences in model performance and for model interdependencies.”
- **Direct support:** A tested framework shows why equal weighting can be inappropriate when models differ in performance and share genealogy/code; it provides a basis for sensitivity analysis of performance-aware ensembles.
- **Caveat:** Case studies are Arctic sea ice/temperature and perfect-model tests. Aggressive tuning can narrow uncertainty unjustifiably; a small provincial sample cannot establish universal weights.
- **Links:** H2, both concepts.

### E05 — NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 provides daily, bias-corrected projections but does not add local physical information

- **Source:** Thrasher, B. et al. (2022), *NASA Global Daily Downscaled Projections, CMIP6*, Scientific Data 9, 262. Official journal page: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01393-4
- **Publication / retrieval:** 2 June 2022 / 29 August 2026.
- **Fingerprint:** “does not add information beyond what is contained in the original CMIP6 scenarios.”
- **Direct support:** The archive contains daily 0.25° projections for 1950–2100 from 35 GCMs and historical/SSP experiments, produced with daily BCSD. It is suitable for daily climate indices and impact screening.
- **Caveat:** The method assumes relative spatial patterns from the reference period remain fixed; statistical downscaling/bias correction does not create convection-scale information. If the attachments use NEX-GDDP, local claims must respect the 0.25° effective scale.
- **Links:** H2, both concepts.

## Findings and verdict

### Confirmed results

- **H2 confirmed.** Bias correction can improve historical agreement but can distort future change signals; QDM/change-preserving diagnostics and out-of-sample validation are essential (E03, E05, E07). Performance-only model selection should be sensitivity-tested against equal weighting and, where feasible, dependence-aware weighting (E04).
- **H3 confirmed at regional scale.** Warming is the more robust signal; rainfall direction is more spatially/seasonally heterogeneous, and precipitation skill is lower than temperature skill (E06–E08). Thailand already shows warming of daily temperature extremes (E09). Province-specific numerical claims still require the attached data.
- **Adaptation relevance confirmed.** Thailand’s NAP explicitly calls for provincial risk profiles and improved extreme-event projections, and identifies Uttaradit among priority natural-resource heat-risk provinces in one scenario assessment (E12). This is a policy rationale, not proof of impact.

### Partially confirmed / narrowed results

- **H1 partially confirmed.** Taylor statistics are incomplete for daily precipitation and daily PDFs reveal wet-day/tail errors hidden by means (E01–E02); studies at different aggregation scales also report different strengths. However, no retrieved study directly proves that the specific Uttaradit model ranking changes from daily to monthly. That must be the paper’s tested hypothesis, not an assumed result.
- **H4 partially confirmed only after narrowing.** No retrieved paper exactly tests daily-to-monthly skill transfer and rank stability for the same CMIP6 models in Uttaradit. A second defensible gap is an Uttaradit-specific seasonal risk-window analysis that combines validated change-preserving correction, temperature and precipitation extremes (if variables exist), scenario/model agreement, and explicit linkage to provincial climate-service needs. Broad claims of “first Uttaradit rainfall study,” “first Northern Thailand drought projection,” or “first Thailand CMIP6 extremes study” are false or unsafe (E10–E16).

### Rejected hypothesis

- **Broad H4 rejected.** Province/local and closely adjacent studies do exist. E13 is directly about Uttaradit; E10–E11 cover Northern Thailand/Nan Basin; E14–E16 use overlapping CMIP6, extremes, Taylor, bias-correction, and adaptation workflows. Novelty must come from the precise question and validation design, not geography alone.

### Article concept A — historical/model-evaluation paper

**Working title (English):** *Does Monthly Skill Imply Daily Skill? A Scale-Aware Evaluation and Robust Selection of CMIP6 Precipitation Simulations for Uttaradit, Thailand*

**Thai title:** *ทักษะรายเดือนสะท้อนทักษะรายวันจริงหรือไม่? การประเมินข้ามสเกลเวลาและการคัดเลือกแบบจำลอง CMIP6 อย่างทนทานสำหรับจังหวัดอุตรดิตถ์*

**Core idea:** Test—not assume—whether model skill and ranking transfer from monthly aggregates to daily rainfall occurrence, intensity, and extremes. The scientific product is a scale-aware selection/weighting protocol for a data-limited monsoon province.

**Research questions / hypotheses:**

1. How concordant are daily and monthly model ranks (Spearman/Kendall, top-k overlap, rank uncertainty)?
2. Do models that reproduce monthly totals/cycles also reproduce wet-day frequency, SDII, upper quantiles, Rx1day/Rx5day, and dry/wet spells?
3. Are rankings stable under split-sample/blocked cross-validation, alternative metrics, and raw versus bias-corrected data?
4. Does a conservative ensemble outperform the selected single “best” model out of sample?

**Minimum defensible analysis:** Taylor diagrams by scale/season; bias and KGE/MAE as complements; daily distribution/QQ/tail metrics; wet-day threshold sensitivity; rank-concordance matrix; block-bootstrap confidence intervals; early/late or k-fold blocked validation; raw vs corrected change-neutral historical comparison; equal-ensemble versus selected/weighted-ensemble skill. If only one station exists, describe this as a site-level Uttaradit case study rather than province-wide mapping.

**Novelty boundary:** Taylor diagrams, bias correction, and selecting a best model are already common in Thailand (E16). Publishability comes from **cross-scale transferability, rank stability, out-of-sample evidence, and distribution/extreme diagnostics**. A null result—rank stability—would still be publishable if tested rigorously.
